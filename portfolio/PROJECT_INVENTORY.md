## Score evidence

### Forward / Insights Recap

- Importance: Client CSMs could not consistently communicate ROI beyond top accounts; manual reporting consumed hours, long-tail customers were underserved, and retention was at risk.
- Ownership: James's firsthand account establishes ownership from concept through MVP and first proposal, then product direction for the first production release; senior-engineer boundaries are explicit.
- Decision richness: Two complete tradeoffs are captured: reject query-backed filtering to preserve narrative focus; reject unreviewed AI output in favor of AI-assisted exploration and deterministic, human-approved scaling.
- Outcome: The proposal became a paid pilot; roughly 13,000 narratives were processed and 9,772 sent after quality gating. Users showed strong sharing/repeat demand; 864 of 1,448 opened narratives completed (60%), and 715 of 864 completed narratives selected annual-repeat intent (83%).
- Visual/interaction quality: One Home asset is reused in multiple placements; broader product evidence is not inventoried.
- Differentiation: Strongest visible AI, product origination, and production-build signal.
- Technical/Design Engineer signal: James owned frontend implementation, narrative AI skill design/development, and the generation pipeline for thousands of narratives; human experience acceptance and managed pre-generation condition evaluation governed quality.
- Unknowns affecting score: Broader shipped evidence, an anonymized condition-path visual, and client-safe wording.

### Juicebox

- Importance: A multi-year product platform for configurable data storytelling.
- Ownership: James specifically drove Slices 2.0 and designed the complete latest Editor UX, from list, panel, and configuration-component systems through prototypes and final refinements.
- Decision richness: Slices 2.0 unified two overlapping filter systems through a backward-compatible Slice model. The Editor redesign supplies a second strong tradeoff: preserve all-at-once scanability or introduce folder-tree navigation and progressively disclosed configuration for platform-scale complexity.
- Outcome: Diverse client apps could be designed and developed within weeks, public examples shipped, and the platform expanded into SaaS authoring. The latest Editor redesign rolled out smoothly and received customer praise; no hard redesign metrics are available.
- Visual/interaction quality: Extensive real interface imagery and GIFs exist.
- Differentiation: Strong complex-systems and data-product evidence.
- Technical/Design Engineer signal: James implemented design-definition prototypes and final frontend UI/UX refinements; frontend engineers owned the production React system implementation.
- Unknowns affecting score: The before/after Editor artifacts, reflection, broader quantitative outcomes, and current product status.

### Finestra Art App

- Importance: Local art marketplace problem with multi-year startup stakes.
- Ownership: James owned the complete app UX/UI and the marketing site's design and engineering; his co-founder owned native iOS engineering.
- Decision richness: James rejected the conventional dimensions-only listing pattern and prioritized wall-scale preview despite limited sensors, believing spatial context would better connect artists with real buyers; it became the most talked-about capability.
- Outcome: More than 60 artists and 200 artworks participated; the team received interest from multiple U.S. cities and alumni-magazine coverage; the startup later closed.
- Visual/interaction quality: Strong breadth of app, wireframe, marketing, and process assets.
- Differentiation: Adds entrepreneurship, consumer mobile, and reflection on failure/closure.
- Technical/Design Engineer signal: James designed and engineered the marketing site and did most Preview web frontend development; his co-founder owned native iOS implementation.
- Unknowns affecting score: Broader business-operation boundaries, press/link verification, and external support before making an absolute “first app” claim.

### Newly discovered recent work

- Local repository history reveals a recent Forward authoring-tool constellation absent from the original inventory: Pipeline Canvas, Forward Media Tools, DataShorts, and Juice Skills.
- Pipeline Canvas is the strongest potential third anchor. Its documented decisions include spatial-first rather than chat-first authoring, a faithful but advisory browser engine, byte-preserving round trips, structured visibility into branching logic, and live folder synchronization.
- James built the complete Pipeline Canvas prototype in collaboration with Claude Code. It has unusually strong POC verification: 37 headless checks and 100/100-row parity with the production pipeline engine.
- James has used Pipeline Canvas locally across a few projects to improve observability of pipeline-project configuration. Because it remains a self-used POC without broader adoption, it is supporting Forward evidence or a Lab artifact rather than the third anchor.
- Forward Media Tools adds deterministic, auditable visual-asset generation, provenance tracking, player-specific validation, and human visual review. It currently reads as supporting evidence for Forward rather than an independent outcome story.
- The deployed Forward Narrative Claude Skill is the selected third anchor. James developed and maintains it entirely; CTO Chris leads Dispatch, and James collaborated with him on the MCP/skill boundary. Other team members use the skill weekly for demo narratives and design prototypes.
- The skill's documented architecture separates narrative judgment and convention enforcement from Dispatch's permissions, persistence, sharing, and authoritative schema validation. It supports portable standalone use and targeted section/group updates through stable IDs.
- Before the skill, James alone created narratives locally and moved them through pull request, merge, and deployment before a URL could be shared—a process that could take multiple days and constrained customized sales demos. The deployed workflow lets the full team create and iterate through Claude Code; production is now the fastest step, shifting attention to the prospect relationship and story.
- James established a continuous-improvement process from teammates' Claude sessions: preserve specific corrections, synthesize recurring patterns, reject one-off preferences, and incorporate generalizable changes into the appropriate layer. The repository journal confirms this process and its preference for better product defaults over accumulating skill prose.
- Demonstrated improvements include responsive chart defaults, coordinated headline and striped-bar emphasis, appropriate photography sourcing, and validation against the product's installed icon set.
- DataShorts demonstrates reusable component work, but its shared ownership and independent outcomes need clarification.
- AI Data Sidekick, Text-to-Movie, Canvas Plan, and Timespan add recent voice-AI, creative-authoring, spatial-AI, and D3/Electron build evidence. None yet has the stakes or outcomes required to displace an anchor.
- GitHub's public repository listing exposes only a small subset of the local work. `timespan` is the clearest recent public standalone repository; most high-value Forward prototypes are private or have no public remote.
