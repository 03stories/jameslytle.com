Starting in 2018, I developed a regular practice of creating a Design Doc for each design task at Juice Analytics. It gave exploration, decisions, and implementation requirements a shared home instead of leaving that context scattered across tickets, sketches, messages, and design files.

## A flexible working format

The document has three main sections: a ticket summary and design brief to establish scope; progress notes for questions, sketches, and evolving ideas; and a Design Solution section for the final requirements, prototypes, assets, components, and success or error states.

![Design Doc template showing the structure for a design task](/content/projects/design-docs/design-doc-template.png)

[View the template PDF](/content/projects/design-docs/1.-design_-ticket-title.pdf)

## From exploration to handoff

For Juicebox work, I used the Design Solution section to walk stakeholders and developers through a proposed experience, then added questions and notes during the discussion. Developers could turn those requirements into implementation tickets, with a link back to the document for context.

![A working Juicebox Design Doc with requirements and product imagery](/content/projects/design-docs/screen-shot-2020-09-29-at-9.57.14-pm.png)

The format supports the design files and prototypes rather than replacing them. Its value is keeping the reasoning and requirements readable as the work evolves.
