# Portfolio Standards

Read this reference when scoring candidates, choosing the featured set, structuring a case study, or evaluating readiness.

## Portfolio-wide signals

The selected portfolio should collectively prove product judgment, complexity, ownership, craft, systems thinking, outcomes, collaboration, technical fluency, range, and taste. A single project need not show every signal. Avoid selecting five variations of the same story.

## 21-point project rubric

Score each criterion from 0 to 3. Record evidence or uncertainty for every score.

### A. Importance of the problem

- 0: Minor or unclear problem
- 1: Useful but routine
- 2: Meaningful product problem
- 3: Strategically important or unusually difficult problem

### B. Personal ownership

- 0: Mostly execution
- 1: Shared contributor
- 2: Significant ownership
- 3: Drove framing, direction, and major decisions

### C. Decision richness

Assess meaningful tradeoffs, constraints, disagreements, failed approaches, and pivots.

- 0: Almost none
- 1: Some
- 2: Several meaningful decisions
- 3: Decisions are central to the story

### D. Outcome

- 0: No known outcome
- 1: Shipped
- 2: Demonstrable user or business improvement
- 3: Strong measurable or strategic impact

### E. Visual or interaction quality

- 0: Difficult to show
- 1: Competent
- 2: Strong
- 3: Exceptional

### F. Differentiation

Assess whether this reveals something the other selected projects do not.

- 0: Redundant
- 1: Some differentiation
- 2: Clearly distinct
- 3: Adds an essential portfolio dimension

### G. Technical or Design Engineer signal

- 0: None
- 1: Some technical collaboration
- 2: Prototype or implementation involvement
- 3: Built, coded, architected, or materially influenced implementation

### Interpretation

A score below roughly 14/21 should usually not become a full case study. A lower-scoring item may still work as a snapshot, lab artifact, writing topic, or archive item. Scores guide judgment; the final set must create the strongest collective story.

## Curation matrix

Aim for distinct coverage. A five-project maximum might include:

1. Flagship: strongest judgment, ownership, and craft
2. Complex systems: workflows, systems, data, enterprise, or scale
3. High craft: exceptional interaction or visual quality
4. Strategy or ambiguity: framing, experimentation, or product direction
5. Hybrid or build: coding, AI, prototyping, or implementation depth

Projects may cover several roles. Stop at three or four if they tell the complete story.

## Documentation levels

### Full case study

Reserve for the strongest three to five projects. Tell a deep decision story.

### Project snapshot

Use for good work that does not justify a full case study. Include one-sentence context, role, two to four images, a short explanation, and a live link when available. Target 30–90 seconds.

### Lab artifact

Use for experiments and smaller builds. Include title, short description, screenshot or video, technology or method, and live demo or repository link when appropriate. Avoid lengthy storytelling.

## Case-study standard

Every major case study must answer:

1. **What was happening?** Give one or two short paragraphs of context.
2. **What was the real problem?** State the tension, stakes, and consequence—not a generic feature need.
3. **What was my role?** Name scope, team, responsibilities, personal ownership, and boundaries.
4. **What were the important decisions?** Center two to four moments using situation → options → constraint → decision → result.
5. **What shipped?** Lead with real screenshots, video, interactive examples, and separate case-study/live-project links when possible.
6. **What happened?** Cover quantitative, qualitative, business, or organizational impact; learning; and what would change now.

## Length and evidence

Target dense reading, not merely short writing: about 4–7 minutes. A useful shape is roughly 150 words of context, 100 words on role and constraints, two to four decision sections, strong visual evidence, and 100–200 words on results and reflection.

Include research, maps, wireframes, workshops, and process artifacts only when they explain an important decision. Prefer shipped evidence over exhaustive process galleries. Label unknowns; never fabricate connective tissue.

## Balance standards

- Major case studies prove senior product judgment and decision-making.
- Live links prove shipped implementation when available.
- Lab items prove curiosity and making across build, interaction, AI, data, or ideas.
- Three to five curated writing pieces prove opinions, judgment, principles, technical understanding, product philosophy, or lessons.
- Minimal UI should make the work dominant and support fast scanning.

## V1 launch checks

- Specific positioning and selected work are understandable quickly.
- Three major case studies are presentable.
- About and resume/contact are complete.
- Required live links work.
- Mobile/responsive behavior works.
- Basic accessibility and performance are credible.
- No broken pages or obvious placeholder content remain in Must Ship.

The portfolio is done when it is credible enough to send to a desired employer—not when every old project, animation, experiment, or visual idea is finished.
